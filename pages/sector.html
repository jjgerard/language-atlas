Northern Ireland: the children's sector

Which public bodies have children's functions, what they publish about spending, and which voluntary and community organisations work alongside them — each with the document it rests on. A first unit, built to be repeated for others.

A dot is an organisation's registered address, not where it works: charities operating across Northern Ireland are registered in Belfast. Trust areas and council areas do not nest — trust boundaries follow the 26 pre-2015 districts — so the two are separate views, never combined.

Who pays, 2024-25

Four layers, each from published accounts and each with its source. They are never added together: a trust's children's spending includes what it pays voluntary organisations, and health does not publish how much, so a total would count the same pounds twice. Every figure is a floor, a published total or a range — and says which.

Social return on investment

A desk study from published data, following the Social Value UK principles as far as a desk study can. It is not an assured SROI: that needs children, families and staff to say what changed for them. There is one ratio per programme and no sector-wide "£X per £1", because programmes change different things. Method: research/children-sector/SROI-METHOD.md.

What the voluntary organisations provide

From each organisation's own documents, coded to one of 18 service areas. The relationship columns count only what a source states; most services have no stated relationship to government, and that is shown, not filled in. Steps in counts statements that a service exists because statutory provision is absent, insufficient or slow — rare, and mostly weak. Click a row to filter the map and the table.

Public bodies with children's functions

Voluntary and community organisations

An organisation is listed only with a documented basis: government children's funding, a contract for a statutory service, membership of a children's network, a directory listing, or being a UK-wide charity working here. Income is the whole organisation's, from its latest register return — not its children's work, so it is not added up.

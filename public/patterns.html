Build a plot: coded categories against a PISA outcome

Coded columns from these maps against the PISA immigrant attainment gap, 2015 to 2025. Read the spread inside a category before the gap between categories — it has been the wider of the two every time.

What kind of rule

Each coded field, by category. A system stating two rules at once is counted under both, so these add up to more than the number of systems.

National systems only. A sub-national unit here would be a country counted once per unit, and the United States alone has 51 — one federal framework read fifty-one times. The maps do include them, so a column can legitimately read a different total there.

A fixed period or a test, and systems say which they are not using

Where an exit rule exists it is one of two things, and they are mutually exclusive by design. New Zealand: “no proficiency-based exit test — capped by duration instead”. Czechia: “capped by prior time in Czech education, not by proficiency”.

Austria runs its twelve months out “regardless of remaining German gaps”. Against that, Puerto Rico and the Northern Mariana Islands exit on a proficiency assessment, and Germany on an assessment of German sometimes referencing a language diploma.

A decision point with nothing behind it

Denmark, Sweden and Finland each fix a review and set no standard for it. Denmark states it outright: the order “sets that decision point but states no criterion for ceasing”.

Three high-capacity systems arrived there independently. That is not the absence of a rule, and it is not a deficiency of context — it is what the exemplars do.

Same context, opposite policy

Austria and Germany are adjacent, share a language of instruction, and have comparable wealth and migrant share. Austria sets a fixed period that expires whether or not the child’s German is adequate. Germany assesses proficiency and sets no period at all.

Whatever decides that, it is not context. The pair is worth more than any correlation over forty-two cases, and it is the reason this page counts rather than scores.

How bilingualism enters the criterion

Sweden requires difficulty in all of a child’s languages, which is what separates a disorder from second-language learning. It is the rule that does the most work.

Greece rules a child out where low attainment “traces to outside linguistic or cultural factors”; Chile excludes for “features of a social or ethnic setting”. Same clause, opposite effect: the bilingual child is screened away rather than assessed.

The typology above is a reading of the entries, not yet a coded pass — the counts are live, the categories are not. Vocabularies for all four fields are in src/coding.js.

When policy changed

Every dated change recorded across all five maps. Years are kept as written, because real ones are ranges and qualifications; the sort uses the first four-digit year in each.

Read the decades as a fact about sources too. More than a third of these rows fall in the 2010s. Some of that is real; some is that a ministry's website goes back about fifteen years, and a 1974 act reaches this table only if somebody republished it.

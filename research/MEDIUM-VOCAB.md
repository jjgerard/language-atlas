# indigenous.mediumOfInstruction

Derived and coded 2026-09-21. 195 national entries, three columns, zero
refusals. Built to close a loop `standing` left open, and the cross-tab it makes
possible is the point of it.

## The finding: what a standing OBLIGES predicts the classroom, what it is CALLED does not

193 units carry both codings. Cross them and `status` — the grand nouns,
official, national, recognised — turns out to predict almost nothing:

```
  standing.status                       n   carries teaching   NOT a medium
  recognised without official status   35        74%               11%
  national, not official               26        69%               23%
  official                             25        68%                8%
  named in policy only                 24        67%               13%
  protected as culture                 10        60%               10%
  none                                 21        19%               48%
```

**Flat.** Every status that exists at all sits between 67% and 74%. An official
language is no likelier to reach a classroom than one merely "named in policy".
Only `none` behaves differently, and that is close to a tautology.

Now cross the same units by what the standing OBLIGES:

```
  standing.force                        n   carries teaching
  enforceable (duty to provide / right) 83        83%
  in between                            75        64%
  does nothing (none / declaratory)     35        23%
```

A 60-point gradient where `status` gave none. **The verb predicts the classroom;
the noun does not.** That is the same lesson `standing` reached from inside its
own column — "`official` is the status most likely to do nothing" — arriving
independently from a different field.

### The confound was tested before this was written

`standing does nothing` and `status: none` overlap: all 21 `none` units are in
the bottom band, which could have made the gradient circular. Excluding them
entirely:

```
  EXCLUDING status = none          n=172
  enforceable                       83        83%
  in between                        75        64%
  does nothing                      14        29%
```

**The gradient survives**, 83 / 64 / 29. But the bottom band falls to 14 units
once the tautological cases go, so the claim rests on those 14 and should be
quoted with that number attached. It is a real effect on a thin base, not a
strong effect.

## The columns

`role` (7 values), `reach` (6), `secured_by` (8). One row is one system.

**`system rule` was the gap the blind test found, and it is the commonest
answer** at 31%. The field's hint proposed "a right, a permission or an observed
practice", and that trichotomy describes how a language is SECURED TO SOMEBODY.
Most systems do not secure it to anybody: Niger's LOSEN art. 19 says the mother
tongue IS the language of instruction in cycle de base 1, and Eritrea, Seychelles,
Tuvalu and Rwanda do the same. Nobody holds a right, nobody elects it, nobody is
observed doing it off their own bat — the instrument simply sets the medium. A
vocabulary built from the hint alone would have forced 60 entries into `right` or
`permission` and meant nothing.

**`who decides` was dropped as a column and folded in as two values.** Measured
across the corpus it appears in 10% of entries and 15 of those 20 are European —
Norway's ten pupils, Belarus's school founder, Austria's *Anmeldeprinzip*. That
is a regional flag, not an axis, the same shape as the provenance rows.
`on request or threshold` (6%) and `left to the school` (3%) carry it inside a
column every entry can answer, and Curacao and Sint Maarten show it is not purely
European.

**`transitional, early exit` is the corpus's own term**, not an imported
typology. Seychelles: "English is the language of instruction from P3 onwards:
EARLY-EXIT TRANSITIONAL". South Sudan and Somalia use it too; Lesotho is
"transitional in form, but the profile names no grade at which the switch falls".
32 entries, 16%. Burkina Faso gives the shape in numbers — the national language
is "90% of year 1, then 80, 50, 20, 10 percent".

## Distribution

```
  ROLE                              REACH                    SECURED BY
  shared medium            31%      not stated      51%      system rule       31%
  not a medium             17%      early primary   14%      none              17%
  sole medium              17%      into secondary  14%      permission        14%
  transitional, early exit 16%      all primary     10%      not stated        13%
  not stated                9%      all levels       6%      right             12%
  support only              6%      preschool only   5%      on request/thresh  6%
  permitted, not implem'd   4%                               practice only      5%
                                                             left to the school 3%
```

**`reach` is the weak column at 51% `not stated`**, and it was kept only after
checking — `extent` on `standing` was built on the same hope and came out 83%.
This one discriminates across five real values on the half of the corpus that
answers, which is worth having, but half is half. Do not read `early primary`'s
14% as a count of early-exit systems; `role` counts those.

## Where it fails

**The grain is `standing`'s grain.** A row is one system and a system may run
several languages at several roles. Montenegro has Albanian as a medium "in most
schools with many Albanian speakers" while "Romani is still not taught at any
level of public education" — one row, two opposite answers, and the dominant
arrangement is coded. Botswana is the same with Setswana against every other
Botswana language.

**Two entries answer a different question.** Iceland and Bosnia are recorded
through Eurydice's CLIL annex — "one of four systems Eurydice records as running
no CLIL at all" — which is about content teaching in a FOREIGN language and says
nothing about an indigenous medium. Both take `not stated` rather than being read
as a finding.

**`support only` and `not a medium` were nearly one value.** They are kept apart
because the corpus insists on the line in its own words: American Samoa has
Samoan "usable ONLY TO FACILITATE teaching English", and Angola's Lei 17/16 art
16(3) "allows Angolan languages as a complement and learning instrument" while
Portuguese "is the medium from the start". A language used to explain is not a
language that teaches.

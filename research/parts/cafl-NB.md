### CA-NB|New Brunswick
STATUS: documented

FRAMING NOTE (read first — NB is the most misreported unit on this map):
French is an OFFICIAL LANGUAGE of New Brunswick, not a foreign language. The
Education Act establishes DUALITY: "Two distinct education sectors are established"
— one organised in English, one in French — and the language of instruction in each
"other than second language instruction, shall be the official language of the school
district". So the second language differs by sector: FRENCH is the second language in
the ANGLOPHONE sector; ENGLISH is the second language in the FRANCOPHONE sector.
Policy 309 applies ONLY to the anglophone sector. Mi'kmaq and Wolastoqey go under
regionalMinorityLanguages.

## Duality — the two sectors (BOTH recorded, as required)

 - field: policyHistory
   quote: "Two distinct education sectors are established consisting of (a) school districts organized throughout the Province in the English language, and (b) school districts organized throughout the Province in the French language."
   source: https://web.archive.org/web/20260520224053/https://laws.gnb.ca/en/showfulldoc/cs/E-1.12/
   note: Education Act s. 4(1), under the heading "LANGUAGE". Source-note on the section: "2010, c.31, s.34".

 - field: policyHistory
   quote: "The language of instruction in schools and classes of a school district, other than second language instruction, shall be the official language of the school district."
   source: https://web.archive.org/web/20260520224053/https://laws.gnb.ca/en/showfulldoc/cs/E-1.12/
   note: Education Act s. 4(3). THE KEY CLAUSE: it carves out "second language instruction" from the otherwise absolute single-language rule, which is what makes French immersion lawful inside an English-language district.

 - field: policyHistory
   quote: "Notwithstanding any other provision of this Act, the educational programs and educational services provided within a school district organized in one official language shall not be provided in the other official language for persons who speak that other official language."
   source: https://web.archive.org/web/20260520224053/https://laws.gnb.ca/en/showfulldoc/cs/E-1.12/
   note: Education Act s. 4(6). Duality is protective, not merely administrative.

 - field: policyHistory
   quote: "For each of the two education sectors established under subsection (1), there shall be a dedicated division within the Department of Education and Early Childhood Development to develop and oversee the implementation and evaluation of educational programs and educational services."
   source: https://web.archive.org/web/20260520224053/https://laws.gnb.ca/en/showfulldoc/cs/E-1.12/
   note: Education Act s. 4(4). Duality runs all the way up into the department.

 - field: policyHistory
   quote: "The purpose of this Act is to recognize (a) that the school system is founded on the principles of free public education, linguistic duality and the inclusion of all pupils, and (b) the importance of the cultures and languages of the Mi'kmaq and Wolastoqey peoples."
   source: https://web.archive.org/web/20260520224053/https://laws.gnb.ca/en/showfulldoc/cs/E-1.12/
   note: Education Act s. 1.1 "Purpose", source-note "2021, c.10, s.1". The purpose section is itself a 2021 amendment.

 - field: primaryRequirement
   quote: "A person entitled to free school privileges under section 8 is entitled (a) if the person has sufficient linguistic proficiency in only one of the official languages of New Brunswick, to receive those privileges in a school district, school and class organized in that official language ... (d) notwithstanding paragraph (a), if the person's parent has rights under section 23 of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, to receive those privileges in a school district, school and class organized in the French language."
   source: https://web.archive.org/web/20260520224053/https://laws.gnb.ca/en/showfulldoc/cs/E-1.12/
   note: Education Act s. 5(1). Sector placement in NB turns on the CHILD'S linguistic proficiency, not only on Charter s.23 status — unusual among the provinces.

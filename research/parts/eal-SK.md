# Language Atlas — `eal` (Majority language acquisition) — Saskatchewan

TERMINOLOGY NOTE: **EAL is the correct word here.** Saskatchewan's Ministry of Education
uses "English as an Additional Language (EAL)" in its own funding manual, its CFR guide and
its newcomer guide. Note also that Saskatchewan's francophone schools run **francisation**
alongside EAL, so the province operates BOTH majority-language routes.

ASSESSMENT NOTE: the Saskatchewan CFR is NAMED and LINKED only. Its A1/A2/B1 sub-level
"Can Do" descriptors are proficiency-level descriptors and are NOT reproduced here, per
brief rule 5. The document also carries an explicit Ministry copyright restriction.

KEY NEGATIVE — THE FUNDING-DURATION HYPOTHESIS FAILS HERE:
 Saskatchewan does NOT fund EAL per pupil and does NOT cap EAL funding at a fixed number
 of years. EAL sits inside a block "Supports for Learning" subcomponent, and the manual
 says in terms that the money is not allocated per student. There is therefore no
 funding-based removal criterion in Saskatchewan, and none should be asserted.

 CONCLUSION: the EAL designation is a MINISTRY POLICY AND FUNDING construct in
 Saskatchewan, not a statutory one. The Act legislates only English, French and
 minority-language instruction. Say this rather than implying a statutory EAL category.

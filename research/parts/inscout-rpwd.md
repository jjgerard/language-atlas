### Answer to the brief's question: YES, it is a specified disability

s.2(zc) verbatim:

> "(zc) 'specified disability' means the disabilities as specified in the Schedule;"

THE SCHEDULE, "[See clause (zc) of section 2] SPECIFIED DISABILITY", under
"1. Physical disability", item D, verbatim:

> "D. 'speech and language disability' means a permanent disability arising out of conditions such as laryngectomy or aphasia affecting one or more components of speech and language due to organic or neurological causes."

**This is the single most important `dld.terminology` finding for India, and it is
a restrictive one.** The statutory category is (a) PERMANENT, (b) organic or
neurological in origin, (c) exemplified by laryngectomy and aphasia - i.e. an
acquired-adult framing. Developmental Language Disorder in a child does not
obviously fall inside it.

The nearest statutory home for developmental language difficulty is instead
under "2. Intellectual disability", item (a), verbatim:

> "(a) 'specific learning disabilities' means a heterogeneous group of conditions wherein there is a deficit in processing language, spoken or written, that may manifest itself as a difficulty to comprehend, speak, read, write, spell, or to do mathematical calculations and includes such conditions as perceptual disabilities, dyslexia, dysgraphia, dyscalculia, dyspraxia and developmental aphasia;"

and item (b), verbatim:

> "(b) 'autism spectrum disorder' means a neuro-developmental condition typically appearing in the first three years of life that significantly affects a person's ability to communicate, understand relationships and relate to others, and is frequently associated with unusal or stereotypical rituals or behaviours."

### On per-state RPwD Rules - NOT TESTED / OPEN

The brief asks whether each state notified its own RPwD Rules and whether they
are findable. I did not get to test this before running out of budget. What can
be said from the Act itself is that it delegates rule-making to the "appropriate
Government", so State Rules do exist in principle. Whether any of them add
anything on speech and language (as opposed to certification procedure and
reservation percentages) is UNVERIFIED and should be treated as an open
question, not assumed either way. My expectation, on the structure of the Act,
is that state RPwD Rules are about certification machinery and would give the
`dld` map little - but that is an expectation, not a finding.

### PA|Panama — fl (Foreign languages in school)
STATUS: documented

HEADLINE: Panama is the one Central American state in this batch whose national English plan IS
backed by a gazetted primary statute with operative curriculum-time numbers in the law itself —
not a press release. This is the positive case against which the other four should be read.

DATE CORRECTION WORTH RECORDING: the task brief floated "Ley 18 de 1997". That is wrong. The
Panamá Bilingüe statute is **Ley 18 de 2017**. The 1990s-era instrument in this chain is Ley 2 de
14 de enero de 2003, whose Article 4 Ley 18/2017 amends.

DRAFT BULLETS:
 - field: primaryRequirement
   bullets:
     - English is compulsory in official and private schools under Ley 2 de 2003
     - Ley 18 de 2017 starts the bilingual method at Kinder and extends it progressively upward
     - Ley 2 de 2003 declares English learning and teaching to be of public interest
 - field: secondaryRequirement
   bullets:
     - At Premedia and Media, Panama Bilingue is delivered "After School", outside the timetable
     - That is an interim arrangement pending the extended school day
     - In-timetable English hours apply at Basica General under the "Kids Program"
 - field: upperSecondary
   bullets:
     - Applies only in designated bilingual centres, not in all schools
     - Designated centres teach all subjects in English except Spanish, Maths, Religion, Civics
     - History and Geography are also excepted from English-medium delivery
     - MEDUCA confers the bilingual-centre category by resuelto under Art. 14
 - field: curriculumTime
   bullets:
     - Statutory floor: English and English-medium areas take at least a third of weekly lesson time
     - At least fifteen hours a week, at a minimum of three hours a day
     - English is designed as instrumental for learning other subject areas
 - field: languagesOffered
   bullets:
     - Ley 18 de 2017 names only English; no other foreign language appears in it
     - Schools may adopt English as a second or third language option under Art. 16
 - field: assessment
   bullets:
     - The statute creates a certification regime for teachers, not for pupils
     - Certification is issued by accredited international certifying bodies
     - No pupil examination requirement appears in Ley 18 de 2017
 - field: teacherSupply
   bullets:
     - Training has two phases: in-country English training then overseas immersion
     - Overseas immersion must be in universities of English-native-language countries
     - Trained teachers owe MEDUCA five years or must repay the cost of their training
     - Certification and recertification earn extra points in appointment and transfer competitions
 - field: higherEducation
   bullets:
     - MEDUCA selects internationally accredited national universities to deliver local training
     - Contracts are formalised through memoranda of understanding
 - field: regionalMinorityLanguages
   bullets:
     - Indigenous languages are native languages in EIB, not foreign languages
     - Act 88 of 2010 recognises the languages and alphabets of Panama's indigenous peoples
     - EIB covers Ngabe, Dule, Embera, Bugle, Wounaan, Naso and Bribri peoples
     - The stated aim is to preserve the native language while promoting Spanish and English

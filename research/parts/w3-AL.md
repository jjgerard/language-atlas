DRAFT BULLETS:
 - field: eal.l1Support
   bullets:
     - Albanian is the only official language, so this is a minority-language entitlement not EAL
     - In Macedonian and Greek minority areas pupils may study their home language
     - That entitlement runs across primary and secondary education
     - It rests on steering documents, not on the legal framework, which recognises one state language
 - field: eal.newcomerCriteria
   bullets:
     - Eurydice records no newcomer criterion for Albania; what it records is a low base rate
     - Under 5 per cent of 15-year-olds speak a different language at home from the school language
     - Under 5 per cent attend language-heterogeneous schools
     - PISA samples for at least one immigrant-background category were under 30 students
 - field: fl.primaryRequirement
   bullets:
     - Starting age is still not recorded: it sits only in a chart, and that gap is kept open
     - Albania is one of seven systems with no policy requiring two foreign languages of all students
     - Compulsory general secondary grades give around 75 taught hours per notional year
     - Albania is outside several Eurydice figures entirely, being listed among the not-covered systems
 - field: dld.terminology
   bullets:
     - Term below is the Albanian COST IS1406 survey translation, not a statutory label
     - Albanian version uses “Çrregullimet Gjuhësore (ÇGJ)”, language disorders
     - Primary form is “Çrregullim gjuhësor Primar”, where the cause is unclear
     - Secondary disorders are those accompanying cerebral palsy, Down syndrome or autism
 - field: dld.serviceModel
   bullets:
     - The Action's own wording in Albanian, not an Albanian service description
     - It says intervention may be delivered by various specialists but usually by language specialists
     - Those are glossed as speech and communication therapists, clinical linguists and psychologists
     - No Albanian institution, caseload or funding route is named in either cited source

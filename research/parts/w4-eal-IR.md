DRAFT BULLETS:
 - field: newcomerCriteria
   bullets:
     - Two separate questions: refugee access, and domestic non-Persian-speaking pupils
     - May 2015 Supreme Leader decree: all Afghan children enrolled whatever their documents
     - Categories are Amayesh card holder, visa holder, or undocumented
     - 2023: 800,080 Afghan and Iraqi pupils enrolled, 380,000 of them undocumented

 - field: l2Support
   prose: |
     Not established from the sources consulted. Persian is the constitutionally required
     language of textbooks and instruction (Art. 15, 1989 Constitution), and the UNHCR Iran
     education page states refugee and undocumented children follow "the same national
     curriculum" as Iranian children, but no source retrieved in this session sets out a
     Persian-as-a-second-language provision: no entitlement, no hours, no designated staff.
     Searched: UNESCO PEER Iran inclusion profile (no language-support section), UNHCR Iran
     education page, the ADSP 2018 review of the Supreme Leader's decree (access only,
     silent on language), and the Center for Human Rights in Iran 2021 report on mother
     languages, which describes teachers in Sistan and Baluchistan teaching Persian from
     scratch in first grade as an unsupported local difficulty rather than a programme.
     Not asserted either way.

 - field: l1Support
   bullets:
     - Permissive and partial; CHRI reports mother-language teaching allowed only in restricted cases
     - Art 15: regional and tribal languages may be taught as literature, in addition to Persian
     - Art 15 also requires all textbooks to be in Persian script
     - June 2016: government ordered Kurdish and Azeri taught in schools in their own regions

 - field: bilingualEducationNotes
   bullets:
     - No dual-medium programme found; Art 15 reserves textbooks and instruction to Persian
     - Regional languages enter as a subject of study, not as a medium
     - Arabic compulsory in all secondary classes and all fields of study (Art 16)
     - PEER: Arabic taught grades 7-12, plus one foreign language in grades 3-12

 - year: 1989
   description: Constitution Arts. 15-16 make Persian the official language and language of textbooks, permit teaching of regional and tribal languages' literature, and make Arabic compulsory in secondary
   source: https://www.constituteproject.org/constitution/Iran_1989
 - year: 2002
   description: Council of Ministers directive allows the Ministry of Education to enrol Afghan children regardless of legal status
   source: https://adsp.ngo/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/IR-15_Afghan-Children%E2%80%99s-Access-to-Education-in-Iran-What-happened-after-the-Supreme-Leader%E2%80%99s-Decree.pdf
 - year: 2004
   description: Directive of the Executive Coordination Council for Foreign Nationals bans enrolment of undocumented foreign nationals and permits fees for Afghan refugee children
   source: https://adsp.ngo/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/IR-15_Afghan-Children%E2%80%99s-Access-to-Education-in-Iran-What-happened-after-the-Supreme-Leader%E2%80%99s-Decree.pdf
 - year: 2015
   description: Supreme Leader's decree (made public 17 May 2015) requires all Afghan children, including undocumented, to be enrolled in Iranian schools
   source: https://adsp.ngo/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/IR-15_Afghan-Children%E2%80%99s-Access-to-Education-in-Iran-What-happened-after-the-Supreme-Leader%E2%80%99s-Decree.pdf
 - year: 2016
   description: Council of Ministers directive of 20 April 2016 nullifies the 2004 directive, creates a special educational support card for undocumented Afghan children and voids tuition-fee articles
   source: https://adsp.ngo/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/IR-15_Afghan-Children%E2%80%99s-Access-to-Education-in-Iran-What-happened-after-the-Supreme-Leader%E2%80%99s-Decree.pdf

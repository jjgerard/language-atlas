DRAFT BULLETS:
 - field: newcomerCriteria
   bullets:
     - Domestic ethnic minorities, not migrants, are the population in question
     - Constitution Art 450: Myanmar is the official language; Art 354(d) protects own language
     - Education review found the language barrier contributes to pupils dropping out
     - Stateless Rohingya are excluded from basic services including education (1982 Act)
 - field: l2Support
   bullets:
     - PEN International reads the 2022 amendments as making Burmese the sole medium
     - NESP promotes Myanmar language via an enrichment programme in some states and regions
     - No separate second-language entitlement, hours or staffing was found in any source
     - The amendment text was not retrievable; PEN International is the source for it
 - field: l1Support
   bullets:
     - PEN reports amendments in force 29 October 2022 cut back the 2014-15 entitlement
     - NEL Art 42 requires state and regional governments to set up ethnic language classes
     - Department of Myanmar Nationalities' Languages leads on ethnic languages, local curricula
     - Per PEN, the amendment confines ethnic language teaching to the primary level
 - field: bilingualEducationNotes
   bullets:
     - PEN reports the 2022 amendments reversed the 2014-15 position described below
     - NEL Art 43 allowed an ethnic language as medium of instruction in basic education
     - Art 44 let regional governments teach ethnic languages from primary upward
     - PEN: first time non-dominant ethnic language teaching was recognised in Myanmar law

POLICY HISTORY ROWS:
 - year: 1982
   description: Citizenship Act denies citizenship to Rohingya, who become effectively stateless and cannot access education
   source: https://education-profiles.org/eastern-and-south-eastern-asia/myanmar/~inclusion
 - year: 2014
   description: National Education Law enacted, allowing an ethnic language as a medium of instruction at basic education level (Art. 43) and requiring ethnic language classes (Art. 42)
   source: https://education-profiles.org/eastern-and-south-eastern-asia/myanmar/~inclusion
 - year: 2015
   description: National Education Law amended; Ethnic Rights Protection Law adopted
   source: https://education-profiles.org/eastern-and-south-eastern-asia/myanmar/~inclusion
 - year: 2022
   description: PEN International reports military amendments to the National Education Law, in force 29 October and published 30 October, that exclude ethnic minority languages as a classroom language at primary level (Art. 43[B]) and limit ethnic language and literature teaching to primary level (Art. 44)
   source: https://www.pen-international.org/news/myanmar-regressive-changes-education-policy-undermine-linguistic-rights-exacerbate-ethnic-divisions

DRAFT BULLETS:
 - field: newcomerCriteria
   bullets:
     - Refugee entitlement is statutory; domestic minority provision has been withdrawn
     - Constitution Art 21: Turkmen is the state language, other native languages guaranteed
     - 2017 Law on Refugees: refugees and asylum seekers access school on the same terms
     - Art 18.8 obliges educational authorities to protect unaccompanied minors
 - field: l2Support
   bullets:
     - No designated second-language service found; Turkmen is simply the universal medium
     - 2013 Law on Education Art 5.2: Turkmen is taught in all education institutions
     - PEER, 2016: only Turkmen was comprehensively taught
     - University entrance exams are in Turkmen, limiting Uzbek and Kazakh pupils' access
 - field: l1Support
   bullets:
     - Statutory right is undercut by practice; the two main minority mediums were closed
     - 2013 Law on Education Art 5.3: state assists study of native languages other than Turkmen
     - Enrolment in Uzbek- and Kazakh-medium schools was stopped from 2000
     - Minority Rights Group: use of Uzbek as a medium of instruction effectively banned
 - field: bilingualEducationNotes
   bullets:
     - Bilingual provision is residual and mostly foreign-partnership schools
     - Art 5.5: non-state schools may use other media but must teach the national component in Turkmen
     - Russian-medium schools shifted to Turkmen; a few Russian-medium classes survive in cities
     - Four Turkmen-Turkish schools teach in Turkish and Turkmen

POLICY HISTORY ROWS:
 - year: 2000
   description: Enrolment of new pupils in Uzbek-medium and Kazakh-medium schools stopped
   source: https://minorityrights.org/communities/uzbeks-4/
 - year: 2001
   description: Russian-medium schools (49 fully Russian-medium and 56 mixed-medium until this point) shift to Turkmen as the language of instruction
   source: https://www.osce-academy.net/upload/file/language_use_and_language_policy_in_central_asia.pdf
 - year: 2013
   description: Law on Education requires Turkmen to be taught in all education institutions, provides state assistance for study of other native languages, and restricts non-state schools' use of other media of instruction (Art. 5)
   source: https://education-profiles.org/central-and-southern-asia/turkmenistan/~inclusion
 - year: 2017
   description: Law on Refugees gives asylum seekers, recognised refugees and persons entitled to special protection access to preschool and general secondary education on the same terms as citizens
   source: https://education-profiles.org/central-and-southern-asia/turkmenistan/~inclusion

NOTES / NEGATIVES:
 - The Turkmen Ministry of Justice document portal (minjust.gov.tm/tk/mmerkezi/doc_view/68) returned
   404 and Refworld's copy of the 2017 Law on Refugees returned 403; the statutory text of neither
   the 2013 Law on Education nor the 2017 Law on Refugees was retrieved. Article numbers above are
   quoted from PEER's account and are marked secondary-source.
 - The OSCE Academy figures date from 2010 and Minority Rights Group's account is undated for its
   most recent statements; the "Bilingual" bullets should be read as historical, not current counts.
 - This entry was, going in, expected to be thin. It is not: PEER and the OSCE Academy paper both
   carry article-level and school-count detail. What is genuinely absent is any second-language
   teaching provision for minority pupils, which no source retrieved describes.

### HK|Hong Kong
STATUS: documented

NOTE ON SCOPE (read before using this): Hong Kong's medium-of-instruction question (EMI vs CMI
secondary schools) is a DIFFERENT question from the foreign-language question and is deliberately
NOT covered here. Nothing below should be read as a statement about MOI. Hong Kong's own framing is
the "biliterate and trilingual" aim, in which English is one of the two written languages and one of
the three spoken ones — the EDB treats English Language as a CORE SUBJECT and (in its own primary
curriculum guide) as "second language" learning, not as a foreign language. The languages that
Hong Kong itself files under "Other Languages" are French, German, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, Urdu
and (from 2028) Hindi.

EVIDENCE:
 - field: primaryRequirement — the policy frame
   quote: "The language education policy of the Government of the HKSAR aims to enable our students to become biliterate and trilingual. We expect that our secondary school graduates will be proficient in writing Chinese and English and able to communicate confidently in Cantonese, English and Putonghua."
   source: https://www.edb.gov.hk/en/edu-system/primary-secondary/applicable-to-primary-secondary/sbss/language-learning-support/featurearticle.html
 - field: primaryRequirement — status of English as a subject
   quote: "The English Language Education curriculum comprises two closely related subjects: English Language (a core subject for Key Stages 1 to 4) and Literature in English (an elective subject for Key Stage 4)."
   source: https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/eng-edu/Curriculum%20Document/Pri_ELCG_Primary_1-6_2025.pdf
   [Key Stage 1 = Primary 1-3, Key Stage 2 = Primary 4-6, Key Stage 3 = Secondary 1-3, Key Stage 4 = Secondary 4-6. So English is a core subject from the first year of primary and there is no starting age or grade at which it "begins" later.]
 - field: primaryRequirement — time
   quote: "The PECG (2024) recommends that, as a core subject, English Language accounts for not less than 16% of the total lesson time at the primary level (Key Stage 1 and Key Stage 2)."
   source: https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/eng-edu/Curriculum%20Document/Pri_ELCG_Primary_1-6_2025.pdf (section 3.7 Flexible Use of Learning Time)
 - field: primaryRequirement — how the guide itself categorises English
   quote: "At the primary level, the motivation for second language learning usually comes through pleasure and enjoyment."
   source: https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/eng-edu/Curriculum%20Document/Pri_ELCG_Primary_1-6_2025.pdf
   [The guide repeatedly says "second language learning", never "foreign language". Recorded here because the atlas asks for the category the source puts it in.]
 - field: upperSecondary — continuation and status
   quote: "This chapter provides the background, rationale and aims of English Language as a core subject in the three-year senior secondary curriculum"
   source: https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/eng-edu/Curriculum%20Document/English%20Language%20Curriculum%20and%20Assessment%20Guide%20(Secondary%204%20-%206)%20(2021).pdf (Chapter 1, Introduction)
 - field: upperSecondary — time
   quote: "As a core subject, English Language accounts for 10.5% – 13% of the total lesson time for the senior secondary curriculum"
   source: same (section 2.5.1 Learning Time)
 - field: upperSecondary — the instrument that reshaped it

   source: same (section 1.1 Background)
 - field: upperSecondary — history of the guide
   quote: "The English Language Curriculum and Assessment Guide (Secondary 4 – 6) was first published in 2007. With the implementation of the New Academic Structure in senior secondary in 2009"
   source: same (section 1.1)
 - field: languagesOffered — what counts as an "other language"
   quote: "Priority will be given to the seven designated OL offered as senior secondary elective subjects, namely: French , German , Hindi , Japanese , Korean , Spanish and Urdu . If there is a need and conditions permit, Arabic and Russian could also be considered."
   source: https://www.edb.gov.hk/en/curriculum-development/major-level-of-edu/secondary/learning-teaching-resources/JSOL.html
 - field: languagesOffered — availability in practice at junior secondary
   quote: "The Chief Executive announced in the 2024 Policy Address that the Education Bureau (EDB) would launch the Pilot Scheme in the 2024/25 school year to encourage eligible schools to apply for additional resources to provide opportunities for junior secondary (JS) students to learn other languages (OL)"
   source: same
 - field: languagesOffered — the funding, which shows the delivery model
   quote: "A one-off funding of $250,000 is available per successful applicant school … Hiring suitable external services for providing the designated OL courses for JS students"
   source: same
   [The scheme buys in outside providers rather than establishing OL teaching posts. This is the paper-versus-practice point for junior secondary: OL is a funded pilot with bought-in tutors, not part of the standing junior-secondary curriculum.]
 - field: languagesOffered — how the OL subjects are actually examined
   quote: "For Category C Other Languages subjects offered in the 2024 HKDSE and before, question papers of the Advanced Subsidiary level from Cambridge Assessment International Education were used."
   source: https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/en/hkdse/assessment/subject_information/category_c_subjects/
 - field: languagesOffered — the 2025 change
   quote: "Starting from the 2025 HKDSE, candidates have to take the stipulated language examinations administered in Hong Kong by the official organisations, which are the official providers of the language examinations concerned. The following subjects are offered: French Language / German Language / Japanese Language / Korean Language / Spanish Language / Urdu Language / Hindi Language (starting from 2028 HKDSE)"
   source: same
 - field: languagesOffered — enrolment is the candidate's own affair
   quote: "Candidates should enroll themselves with the examination administrators of the stipulated language examinations according to the schedule of the respective language examinations."
   source: same
   [This is the sharpest paper-versus-practice fact for Hong Kong: the seven "Other Languages" appear on the HKDSE, but the HKEAA does not set or administer them; the candidate registers with an outside language body. Hindi is listed but has no examination until 2028.]

DRAFT BULLETS:
 - field: primaryRequirement
   bullets:
     - EDB frames English as a second language and core subject, not as a foreign language
     - English Language is a core subject from Key Stage 1, i.e. Primary 1, with no later start
     - PECG 2024: English should be not less than 16% of total primary lesson time
     - Policy aim is "biliterate and trilingual": Chinese and English written, plus Putonghua
 - field: upperSecondary
   bullets:
     - Continues as one of the four compulsory senior secondary core subjects, S4 to S6
     - English takes 10.5% to 13% of senior secondary lesson time under the 2021 guide
     - EDBCM No.39/2021 optimised the four core subjects, effective from S4 in 2021/22
     - Any second language beyond English is elective, taken as an HKDSE Category C subject
 - field: languagesOffered
   bullets:
     - Seven designated other languages: French, German, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, Urdu
     - From 2025 HKDSE these are set by outside language bodies, not by the HKEAA
     - Candidates must enrol themselves with the external examiner; Hindi has no exam until 2028
     - Junior secondary OL is a funded pilot buying in external tutors at $250,000 per school

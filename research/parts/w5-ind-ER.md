NOTES ON WHAT WAS SEARCHED AND NOT FOUND:
 - Eritrea has no education act, according to the UNESCO PEER profile, and none was found in this session. The Education Sector Plan and the National Education Policy, not legislation, are the instruments here.
 - The Eritrea Ministry of Education National Curriculum Framework was NOT retrieved in this session. Consequently: whether a mother tongue is timetabled as a SUBJECT at middle or secondary level, how many periods a mother tongue receives at primary, and how a school's mother tongue is chosen where a community is mixed are all "Not established from the sources consulted". Searched: the full text of the 2018 Education Sector Plan, the UNESCO PEER Eritrea inclusion profile, and web search for a Ministry of Education curriculum framework PDF.
 - The 2018 Sector Plan does NOT enumerate which of the nine languages is used in which school or region for elementary instruction. It gives an adult-literacy participation table by ethnic group covering all nine, but nothing equivalent for elementary schooling.
 - The 1997 Constitution's status is contested and is not asserted here beyond quoting its Article 4(3) text and PEER's note that Eritrea has no education act.
 - Arabic appears in the plan only as a teacher-training line ("Conduct training on Arabic language proficiency for 150 Arabic [teachers]"). Its curricular status was not established.
 - The UNESCO PEER Eritrea ~curriculum tab returns 404.

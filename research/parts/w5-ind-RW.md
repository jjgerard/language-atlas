POLICY HISTORY CANDIDATES:
 - year: 2008
   description: "A Ministry of Education strategy document moves the school system decisively from French to English: 'English language shall be a medium of instruction ... except in lower primary (P.1, P.2 and P.3) where the medium of instruction shall be Kinyarwanda' (as quoted by UNICEF ESA 2017)"
 - year: 2012
   description: "Law nº 23/2012 of 15/06/2012 governing the organization and functioning of nursery, primary and secondary education leaves the language of instruction to be fixed by an order of the Minister"
 - year: 2015
   description: "Ministry of Education and Rwanda Education Board publish the Competence-Based Curriculum framework, pre-primary to upper secondary, setting Kinyarwanda as the language of learning in pre-primary and lower primary and English from upper primary onwards, with Kinyarwanda a compulsory subject at every primary level"
 - year: 2015
   description: "Constitution of 2003 as revised in 2015, Article 8: Ikinyarwanda is the national language and, with English and French, an official language"
 - year: 2019
   description: "Ministry of Education communiqué following a consultative meeting of 2 December 2019 endorses English as medium of instruction in lower primary (P1-P3) as well as upper primary (P4-P6), for private, public and government-aided schools alike"
 - year: 2021
   description: "Law nº 10/2021 of 16/02/2021 determining the Organisation of Education, Article 20, makes English the medium of instruction by statute, with official languages taught as subjects; Article 137 repeals Law 23/2012, Law 44/2015, Law 01/2017 and Law 36/2018 and all contrary provisions"

NOTES ON WHAT WAS SEARCHED, AND ON CONFLICTS:
 - The 2015 REB framework and the 2021 law say different things about lower primary. The law is later and repeals contrary provisions; the framework has not been visibly reissued. Both are quoted above so the atlas need not resolve it in its own voice.
 - The constituteproject text used is the 2003 constitution as revised in 2015 and does not include any later amendment; it lists three official languages and does not mention Kiswahili. No claim about Kiswahili's official status is made here.
 - Law nº 10/2021 was read in full for language provisions: apart from Article 20, "language" occurs only in the article listing the responsibilities of the Teachers' Council (article number not recorded here) and in the description of the secondary cycles. There is no minority-language, mother-tongue or regional-language entitlement anywhere in the Law.

# Negative triage, batch A — `indigenous.mediumOfInstruction`

85 entry-fields, all carrying `role = not a medium` (three of them without a
`secured_by`; the other 82 carry `secured_by = none`).

Grades follow the maintainer's rule of 2026-09-28: a negative is worth recording
when it says what the negative IS. Where all that can honestly be said is that a
search came up empty, the value belongs at `not stated`.

**Counts: A 27 · B 52 · C 6.**

## A — a source states the absence, or entails it (27). Keep the coded value.

Twenty-three of these are entailments of the positive kind: the entry states
what the medium IS, and that settles that the language in question is not it.

- **AE | United Arab Emirates** — A — "Arabic is the language of instruction for all subjects in government schools". Government schools are settled; the private sector is enumerated by syllabus ("IB, British, American, French, German, Indian"), none of them an indigenous language, so the enumeration carries it rather than leaving a hole.
- **BE | Belgium — French Community** — A — "CLIL pairs French with Dutch or German, both state languages, ISCED 1-3". The languages immersion may run in are enumerated, and a closed list excludes the regional languages; bullet 2 ("That is state-language immersion, not regional-language provision") is the coder's gloss on that list, not a second source. The weakest A in the group — see the note at the end on the CFWB regional-languages FAQ, which is cited and not reported from.
- **BG | Bulgaria** — A — "No teaching IN minority languages exists; Art 13(2) ZPUO makes Bulgarian the medium". Both halves: a statute that sets the medium, and Art 13(3)-(4) that allows subjects "in a FOREIGN language only, never a minority language".
- **BH | Bahrain** — A — "Arabic is the language of instruction in all public schools", with the private sector's languages named too ("English, some in Arabic or French").
- **CA | Yukon** — A — "No provision makes a First Nation language the medium for other subjects". The subject of that sentence is the provisions, not the search: it says what the provisions do, and bullet 2 says what they do instead ("Revitalization runs 'through classroom instruction' and on the land").
- **CN | Anhui** — A — "Schools and other educational institutions must use Putonghua as basic teaching language".
- **CN | Beijing** — A — "Schools must use Putonghua as the basic language in teaching, meetings and group activities".
- **CN | Hubei** — A — "Schools, kindergartens and other institutions must use Putonghua as basic teaching language".
- **CN | Hunan** — A — "Schools in the prefecture shall use the national common language and script for teaching". Note the grain: the bullet is about the autonomous prefecture, and the entry is the province.
- **CN | Jiangsu** — A — "Putonghua is the basic language of teaching in Jiangsu schools under the 2006 measure", and bullet 4 states the scope ("Applies province-wide").
- **CN | Shanxi** — A — "Schools and other educational institutions must use Putonghua as basic teaching language".
- **CV | Cape Verde** — A — "DL 27/2022 art 13: Portuguese is 'lingua nao materna' but the school's lingua veicular". The instrument names the vehicular language. See the note below: bullet 1 arguably describes a confined medium role rather than none at all.
- **GA | Gabon** — A — "French has continued to be the only language of instruction in all schools".
- **GQ | Equatorial Guinea** — A — "Spanish is the medium of instruction and a compulsory subject, spoken by over 85%". Bullet 1 does the work; bullet 2 ("Local languages seem confined mainly to the family sphere") is a hedge and would not have carried it alone.
- **IN | Manipur** — A — "No tribal language is a medium: only English and Manipuri, Class I to X". The media are enumerated, and bullet 3 says what the tribal languages are instead ("subjects, never the medium").
- **IN | Tripura** — A — "No minority language is a medium at primary, upper primary or secondary (state return, 2014-15)", with higher secondary covered by bullet 2. Every stage is accounted for, and the source is the state's own return.
- **IN | Uttar Pradesh** — A — "U.P. Board Notification 1952 grants recognition only for education through Hindi". The instrument was read and its effect stated; bullets 3 and 4 ("no Urdu-medium provision is reported", "State furnished no data") are sourcing reports and are not what supports the value.
- **IR | Iran** — A — "In schools the permitted use is teaching their literature, not instruction through them".
- **KW | Kuwait** — A — "Arabic is the only language of instruction at every level".
- **LU | Luxembourg** — A — "All instruction is in a language other than Luxembourgish".
- **MC | Monaco** — A — "Lessons are taught in French - Monegasque is a subject, not a medium".
- **SA | Saudi Arabia** — A — "UNESCO's country profile: only classical Arabic is used in primary schools". Not rounded up: bullet 2 says "Private schools may use a language other than Arabic as a medium" and does not say which, so the private sector is unsettled and the A rests on the public primary statement.
- **SM | San Marino** — A — "Teaching runs in Italian, with English and other European languages used alongside it".
- **ST | São Tomé and Príncipe** — A — "Portuguese is used in all acts of the State, in education and in the media".
- **SX | Sint Maarten** — A — "School board picks the medium, but only English or Dutch are allowed". A closed list, so the creole is excluded by the rule and not merely absent from it.
- **TN | Tunisia** — A — "The State has always refused to bring Berber into the education system"; bullet 2, "Berber speakers get no teaching in their mother tongue", says the same thing from the pupil's side.
- **YE | Yemen** — A — "UNESCO: Arabic is the only language of instruction in public schools, primary to university". Bullet 3 ("No conditional or local exception ... was found in the source consulted") is a search report, but it is not what supports the value.

## C — the prose says neither; somebody has to go back to the source (6)

- **CN | Ningxia** — C — the strongest bullet is "Schools must make Putonghua and standard characters part of quality education", which is a promotion duty, not a rule setting the medium; the other two are about reporting and inspection. **Check:** whether the 2020 Ningxia language regulation (nxrd.gov.cn) carries the "basic teaching language" clause the other six Chinese entries quote, and whether the autonomous-region regulations preserve any minority-language medium.
- **IN | Arunachal Pradesh** — C — "No tribal language appears as a medium anywhere in the state's returns" is undercut by bullet 3, "State furnished no data on facilities at any stage of school education": a nil return is not a finding. Bullet 4, "CLM urges primary education in mother tongues; no such scheme exists", would be an A if the Commissioner says it, and nothing else if the coder inferred it. **Check:** whether the 51st/52nd CLM report itself asserts that no mother-tongue primary scheme exists in Arunachal Pradesh, or whether that sentence is the drafter's reading of the empty return.
- **IN | Sikkim** — C — "No mother tongue is a medium at upper primary, secondary or higher secondary (51st Report)" is sourced and sound for those stages, but bullet 2 says "Nothing at all furnished about the primary stage, medium or subject" and bullet 5 that "The state made no return at all to the 52nd Report". Primary is exactly where a mother-tongue medium would sit. **Check:** the primary stage — Sikkim's own Bhutia, Lepcha and Limboo provision (the state source in the citation list is about master's courses in those three languages, not about school medium).
- **SD | Sudan** — C — "The constitutional guarantee does not reach school-level medium of instruction" establishes that the constitution is silent, not that the system has no provision; bullet 2 is about higher education only. **Check:** the General Education Act and the UNESCO profile's basic-education section for any mother-tongue medium clause at basic level.
- **SL | Sierra Leone** — C — the single bullet, "The sector plan concedes English is the only language promoted", is about promotion, not about medium, and one bullet on promotion cannot carry a finding about the classroom. **Check:** the Education Act 2004 and the basic-education policy (both already in the citation list) for a lower-primary mother-tongue medium provision.
- **US | Louisiana** — C — "No French medium-of-instruction requirement appears in R.S. 17:272", with bullet 2 stating correctly that "The duty falls on what schools must OFFER, not on what they teach in". That is a real reading of a named instrument, but the instrument is about what must be offered, so its silence cannot settle the question, and Louisiana is the one US unit in this batch where a French-medium arrangement plainly exists outside the statute read. **Check:** the CODOFIL immersion programme and Bulletin 741 / LAC 28 for the medium-of-instruction rules governing French immersion. Graded C rather than B because a named instrument really was read; graded C rather than A because the instrument read is the wrong one for the question.

## What the batch shows

**The field's negatives are not sound, and the reason is concentrated rather
than diffuse.** 52 of 85 are B, but 48 of those 52 are US states, and all 48
were coded from the same four sources — two ECS graduation-requirement
comparisons, the Seal of Biliteracy report and an American Councils
foreign-language enrollment survey. Not one of those documents is about medium
of instruction. So the whole US block is a single sourcing decision repeated 48
times, and it has been read off the map as 48 findings about 48 school systems.
The shape is the one already recorded for the policy-history provenance column:
a value that turned out to measure where the project looked rather than what the
world does.

That matters because several of these states are not plausibly `not a medium`.
Alaska, Montana, New Mexico, Arizona, Oklahoma, Washington, Wisconsin, Minnesota
and the Dakotas all run indigenous-language immersion schooling of some kind.
Moving them to `not stated` does not assert that provision exists; it stops the
map asserting it does not.

**Outside the US block the field is in good shape.** Of the 37 non-US units,
27 are A, 6 are C and only 4 are B. The A cases are overwhelmingly of one type:
the entry states what the medium IS — Arabic, French, Portuguese, Putonghua,
Spanish, Italian — and that settles the question without anyone having to make a
claim about their own searching. That is the healthiest form this value takes,
and it is worth saying that the seven Chinese provincial entries are among the
cleanest in the batch: an instrument that says schools must use Putonghua as the
basic teaching language is exactly the "instrument was read and makes no
provision" case, stated from the positive side.

**The C cases share one shape.** Five of the six (Ningxia, Arunachal Pradesh,
Sikkim, Sudan, Sierra Leone) are entries where a real source was read but it
answers an adjacent question — promotion, recognition, higher education, a stage
other than the one that matters, or a return that was never filed. Louisiana is
the sixth and the clearest: a statute read correctly, about the wrong duty.
None of the six should be moved blind in either direction.

### Glosses that now overclaim

1. **`not a medium` names its own counter-example.** The gloss reads
   "Somebody checked and the language does not carry teaching at any stage -- a
   finding, not a silence", which is precisely today's rule, and then offers as
   one of its four exemplars Qatar: "No language other than Arabic is recorded
   as a medium in the sources." Under the rule that sentence is a B. Mauritania,
   another of the four ("records no use of Fula, Soninke or Wolof as a medium at
   any stage"), is at best borderline — it is a report on what a profile
   contains. Egypt and Bulgaria hold up. The gloss's *definition* is right and
   half its *evidence* is not, which is the more dangerous failure of the two,
   because the exemplars are what a coder actually pattern-matches against.
   Recommend swapping Qatar out for a positive-entailment exemplar — Kuwait's
   "Arabic is the only language of instruction at every level" or Tunisia's
   "The State has always refused to bring Berber into the education system" —
   so the gloss shows the shape it is asking for.

2. **`secured_by = none` overclaims harder than `not a medium` does.** Its gloss
   is "Established that nothing secures it", with "Established" doing the work,
   and 48 US units carry it on the strength of four sources that never addressed
   the question. `not a medium` at least admits in its own wording that it is a
   finding somebody has to make; `none` asserts the finding was made. Every one
   of the 52 B units carries `none`, and after this recode that value loses
   roughly a third of its population — worth checking what it looks like on the
   remaining batches before anyone reads a distribution off it.

3. **A smaller inconsistency worth a line.** `MEDIUM_SECURED_BY['left to the
   school']` cites Sint Maarten ("the same arrangement" as Curaçao) as its
   exemplar, while the Sint Maarten entry in this batch is coded
   `not a medium` / `none`. Both can be true — the board's choice is between
   English and Dutch, and the creole is excluded from that choice — but a reader
   going from gloss to entry will find the exemplar coded as something else.
   The gloss could say which language it is talking about.

## What the batch shows

**The B's are one sourcing decision, not forty.** 34 of the 43 B's are US states
resting on the same two documents — the 2024 National Seal of Biliteracy report
and the ECS 50-state graduation-requirements inventory. Neither asks what is
taught or what standing a language holds; one records awards from states that
chose to report, the other records graduation credits. Every one of those
entries says so in its own second bullet ("Rests on a Seal survey and a 50-state
inventory, not on state statute"). The count is a fact about how the United
States was sourced, and reading 34 `not taught` cells as a finding about
American schools inverts it — the states that DO teach Indigenous languages are
mostly outside this batch, so what is left is the residue of one survey.

**The A's are four recognisable shapes, and all four are worth keeping.** An
instrument read and found empty (Quebec's Régime pédagogique tables, Sint
Maarten's art. 11, PEI's Programs of Studies); a profile or inventory that runs
through the ground and names none (Eurydice Fig. A1, PEER on Qatar); a
monitoring body recording that a state has not acted (the CLM on Rajasthan and
Puducherry); and a state's own declaration to a treaty body (San Marino, Uttar
Pradesh, the Andamans). The last shape always needs its qualifier carried: in
all three cases the entry already records that the declaration is the state's
and that the monitor did not adopt it.

**Two bullets can sit on one entry and answer different fields.** Oman and Yemen
carry a real sentence — "Named as spoken languages, not as school subjects" —
and a retrieval failure. The real sentence answers `taughtAsSubject`; the field
being coded was `standing`, where only the retrieval failure was available.
`STANDING_STATUS` warns against exactly this borrowing, and the B is the honest
outcome. Tunisia is the mirror: its `taughtAsSubject` is a clean A on two
sources and its `standing` is a B on a profile the entry itself measures at two
sentences.

**Eleven entries are worth a substantive look whatever the grade.** ⚑ Ireland,
Luxembourg and Malta carry `standing.status = none` under the Eurydice formula
while their own or their neighbours' bullets record Irish, Luxembourgish and
Maltese as state languages; the Belgian French Community carries it while its
fourth source is a Wallonia-Brussels page on *langues et cultures régionales*;
Türkiye carries it while a MEB elective page for *yaşayan diller ve lehçeler*
sits in the source list; Puducherry carries it above its own bullet "Four
official languages"; Uttar Pradesh and the Andamans carry it above documented
academies and Bengali/Tamil/Telugu-medium provision; Luxembourg and Malta carry
`localTerm.family = none in use` on bullets that name a state language, which is
a *category*, not the absence of one; and Georgia and Venezuela are the two C's
where the coded value looks positively wrong. None of these is a
search-versus-source problem, so none of them moved in `recode-C.json`.

## The 48 `none in use` cells on `indigenous.localTerm.family`

**The brief's premise does not hold, and neither does the gloss's.** Counted in
`data/indigenous.json` today the value sits on 48 entries, of which **33 are
United States states** — not 47. The gloss in `src/coding.js` says "47 of its 51
entries", which is stale on both numbers.

**More importantly, none of the 33 is in this batch, or in any batch.** Batch C
holds 10 `localTerm` rows and batches A, B, D and E hold none at all, so the
cells the warning is about were never put up for grading. I could not grade them
individually from the file I was given, so I read their stored prose instead —
no fetching, just `data/indigenous.json`.

**They rest on one pair of sources, and they grade B on their own wording.** All
33 lead with "No state term for an Indigenous or regional language was found",
and every one carries a bullet conceding the basis: "Rests on a Seal survey and
a 50-state inventory, not on state statute", or "No Seal language list was
filed, so even that inventory is missing" (7 states). That is the subject of the
sentence being the checking, twice over — the searcher failed, and the entry
says the documents searched were the wrong documents. By today's rule all 33
belong at `not stated`. They are not in `recode-C.json`, which is batch C's B's
only; they need a batch of their own, or a single sweep.

The 10 that ARE here do **not** rest on one source. Seven (BA, BE, IS, LI, LU,
MT, TR) rest on Eurydice Key Data Fig. A1, which is an enumeration of what each
system names and therefore carries a real absence; three (DO, KW, QA) rest on
PEER country profiles. Nine graded A and one (Kuwait) graded B. So the value's
48 cells split cleanly: a two-thirds US-state block that is a sourcing artefact
and should move, and a European/Gulf remainder that is mostly genuine.

## The `standing.status = none` gloss

The gloss reads: *"Somebody looked and there is no standing — NOT the same as
nobody having looked."* Its subject is the looking, and by the maintainer's rule
today that is the wrong voice.

**The entries carrying it support a stronger, source-based wording, and they say
what it should be.** Of the 23 `standing` rows here, 17 graded A, and every one
of those 17 names a source that does the asserting: an instrument read and
conferring nothing; an inventory or profile that runs through recognition and
names none (Eurydice Fig. A1, PEER); a monitoring body recording that the state
has not acted (the CLM on Rajasthan's unnotified threshold areas, on
Puducherry's non-recognition); or the state's own declaration to a treaty body
(San Marino, Uttar Pradesh, the Andamans). A wording such as —

> A source that covers the ground attaches no standing: an instrument was read
> and confers none, an inventory or profile that runs through recognition names
> none, a monitoring body records that none was conferred, or the state itself
> has declared there is none. Where a state's declaration is the basis, the
> entry says so and says whether the monitoring body adopted it. Distinct from
> `not stated`, which is where the only thing that can be said is that a search
> came up empty.

— would be carried by all 17 without stretching any of them, and would have
excluded the 6 B's on its face. Those 6 are the argument for the change: Oman,
Yemen and the three US states reached `none` on retrieval failures alone, and
"somebody looked" is precisely the phrase that let them in.

One thing the new wording should keep, because three entries here need it: the
San Marino qualifier. A state's declaration is a source statement, but San
Marino's entry has to be able to record that it was "Said in a state report
under FCNM art. 25(1), not in any San Marino instrument" and that the commitment
is "called only a hypothetical commitment" — the declaration is the evidence,
and the monitor's non-endorsement is part of it.

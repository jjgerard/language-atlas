### no `not stated` available

`TEST_TYPES`, used by `dld.assessments.test_type`, has six values and **none of them is
`not stated`**: battery, single task, screener, criterion checklist, access arrangement,
none. So the eight `dld.assessments` B rows above have nowhere honest to go. The only
negative the vocabulary offers is `none`, whose gloss ("the entry establishes that no
instrument is named or in use") is exactly the claim these entries cannot support. They
are left out of `recode-E.json` for this reason as well as for row grain. A `not stated`
value needs adding to `TEST_TYPES` before this batch's assessments B rows can be written.

## What the batch shows

**Two thirds of these negatives survive.** 65 of 104 are backed by a source that states the
absence or enumerates the ground and names none — a licensing rule that lists the
professions (American Samoa), a staffing list for the only health provider on the islands
(Wallis and Futuna, where "there is no private medical practice on the islands" makes the
list exhaustive and the entailment explicit), a ministry norms document that says the
profession does not yet exist and reserves a place for it (Burundi), a federal return
reporting zero (Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Palau).

**The B cases cluster by SOURCE, not by field.** Nine of the 26 come from two documents:
the crowd-sourced West Africa practitioner directory that "invites correction" (Gambia,
Liberia, Sierra Leone — seven cells) and a regional ENT survey whose *country list* was
read as a finding about the countries it omits. Absence from a survey's sample is absence
of a datum. The ENT-survey omission argument appears on six entries and stands alone on
none of the A ones.

**"Source describes special-needs provision generally, never language disorder"** is
boilerplate on 17 entries, and it is an admission that the source does not reach the
column. Where a *later* bullet states the absence positively — Algeria, "Algeria does not
have an education monitoring report"; Gabon; Malawi; South Sudan outcomes; Tunisia,
"Tunisia lacks standardized tests" — the entry is an A despite the boilerplate. Where no
later bullet does, it is a B. That split, 9 A against 8 B under the same opening
sentence, is the whole distinction the rule draws, visible in one phrase.

**How I graded term counts.** A zero count never carried an A on its own. Syria
("'speech' and 'therapist' each return 0 hits"), Bhutan ("both return 0 hits") and
Catalonia ("'logoped' 0 in 341,554 chars") are all downgraded. Catalonia is the Libya
error repeated in miniature and worth naming: the count searched for a **profession**
word in order to conclude that no **disorder** term exists, and a Catalan disorder term
("trastorn del llenguatge") would not be caught by it. The Vatican is the one place a
count was allowed to stand, and only as corroboration: the deciding bullet is that the
Holy See's own CRC report, which answers concluding observations on education and health,
"names no disorder category, service or school-language provision anywhere in it", and
the entry ran a sanity check ("'Holy See' 136, 'Vatican' 15, 'school' 16") proving it had
read the right text.

### Verdict on `no programme found`

**Thirteen entries carry it. Seven survive a strict reading; six do not.**

- Survive (A): **BI|Burundi** (ministry norms list orthophonie as not yet present),
  **CD|DR Congo** ("only an introductory unit at a Kinshasa university" — states what is
  there), **CM|Cameroon** ("No university training programme in orthophonie exists"),
  **HT|Haiti** ("No module on inclusive or special education in universities or teacher
  training colleges"), **MW|Malawi** ("The only allied training listed is audiology"),
  **SN|Senegal** ("No public school of orthophonie, and a university module only
  mooted"), **SZ|Eswatini** ("No medical school and no training programme of any kind in
  either survey year").
- Do not (B): **BZ|Belize**, **CG|Congo**, **GM|Gambia**, **LR|Liberia**, **SD|Sudan**,
  **SL|Sierra Leone**.

So the corrected gloss is now *accurate about its own name* and *wrong about a majority
of its members*. The value is named for the search, and seven of thirteen entries carry
evidence far stronger than a search. Burundi's is a government instrument positively
stating the profession does not exist in the country, which is about as strong as this
corpus gets, and it sits under a label saying nobody could find anything.

The right fix is not to regrade those seven down but to **split the value**: keep
`no programme found` for the six B cases — or route them to `not stated`, which is what
this batch does — and add a positive value, `no programme established`, parallel to
`none established` on SERVICE_SECTOR, for the seven. Until that exists, applying
`not stated` to the six leaves `no programme found` holding only entries whose evidence
contradicts its name.

### Glosses that overclaim against the entries carrying them

1. **`TEST_TYPES.none`** — "The entry establishes that no instrument is named **or in
   use**". Fourteen of the 21 assessments A rows say the opposite. New South Wales
   requires "at least one standardised language test"; Michigan requires "not fewer than
   two standardised instruments"; Oregon "needs a language sample plus standardized
   tests"; Louisiana "requires standardized tests or published normative data"; Alberta,
   British Columbia, Quebec, Minnesota, New Hampshire and Utah say much the same.
   Instruments are plainly **in use** — the rules simply do not name which. `none` is
   doing two jobs, "no instrument exists" and "the rule names none", and the second is a
   fact about drafting style, not about the system. An `unnamed instrument required`
   value would separate Togo (regional prose, nothing in use) from Michigan (two
   instruments mandated, unnamed).
2. **`TERM_TYPE.none located`** — the value name is a search, like `no programme found`,
   and its gloss ("Somebody looked and found no word of any kind") makes the searcher the
   subject. Bhutan is listed in that gloss as an example and is a C here, because its own
   entry names speech therapists.
3. **`OUTCOME_EVIDENCE.none established`** — "Somebody looked and there is no outcome data
   **of any kind**". The United States carries it beside "Federal reporting covers IDEA
   categories in aggregate". The gloss's own examples (Barbados, Dominica, Haiti, Saint
   Vincent) are small jurisdictions where it is true; it does not survive being applied to
   a system that publishes aggregate data but not DLD-specific data.
4. **`WORKFORCE_ENTRY.none named`** — "the entry establishes that **nobody is qualified or
   designated** to do it". Samoa carries it while naming a volunteer speech language
   therapist on the ministry's specialist team, and Saint Vincent carries it beside an
   "acute shortage of trained staff, including therapists". Both describe an entry route
   that is unusual (overseas volunteer placement) or scarce, not absent.

## Shapes checked before writing recode-E.json

`sectors` (SERVICE_SECTOR) and `evidence_found` (OUTCOME_EVIDENCE) are list-capable
columns, but on every unit in the recode they are stored as a **bare string**
("none established"), so the recode writes bare strings to match. The workforce and
terminology columns are single-valued everywhere. No cell in the recode is wrapped in an
array. `dld.assessments` and `dld.legalEntitlement` are `many: true` in `src/coding.js`
and are excluded from the JSON entirely; `legalEntitlement` had no B verdicts in this
batch, and the eight assessments B verdicts are listed above.
